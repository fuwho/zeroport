Identity-native zero trust

The safest port is the one you never opened.

Every VPN gateway, firewall and bastion has to listen on a public address. That is how attackers find them — and everything after that is a race between their scanners and your patches.

ZeroPort doesn't guard that door. It removes it. Nothing listens, so there is nothing to scan — and the people who belong still connect in seconds, at full speed.

01 — The paradox

Scan it. You'll find nothing.

A scanner sweeps the internet looking for something that answers. A ZeroPort service answers nothing, because nothing is listening. Which raises the obvious question: how does anyone legitimate ever get in?

01Try it from both sidesIllustration
# ready when you are…

Why this isn't port-knocking. Port-knocking and single-packet authorization still run a daemon that listens for the knock — there is still a surface, and still a packet to replay or fuzz. ZeroPort has no listening surface to address at all.

02 — How it works

Four steps, and then the middleman leaves.

Two machines that can't be dialled still have to find each other. They're introduced briefly through a rendezvous — then they talk directly, and the introducer drops out of the path completely.

03 — Why it's safe

Three guarantees you can test yourself.

Removing the door isn't enough on its own. These are the properties that make the result trustworthy — and each one is a mechanism, not a promise.

01No single administrator can betray itIllustration

The roster is signed by a key split across three officers. Pick who signs, and watch what the network does about it.

Select at least one officer.
02Access expires on its ownIllustration

Authorization is a short lease, not a permanent membership. It has to be renewed while a device stays healthy and reachable.

So a stolen laptop, switched off in a bag, loses its access by itself. Nobody has to notice. Nobody has to press revoke.

And you stop guessing who did what. Because every peer holds a cryptographic identity, a log line reads officer-laptop → national-system:5432 — not 10.0.4.12 → 10.0.9.3. Names, not addresses you have to go and look up.

04 — Evidence

Everything above is built, and it's tested.

The widgets on this page illustrate how the system behaves. The system itself is real code with no third-party dependencies, and it runs from a terminal on an ordinary machine.

Said plainly. This page is a teaching tool, not a running network — the widgets illustrate the mechanisms rather than execute them. The working system, its test output, and an operations console driven by real data are all in the repository. One gap remains: everything runs on a single machine, so NAT traversal and wide-area latency aren't demonstrated yet.
